# 7 May 2026

Following yesterday's GPT-5.5 memory-and-spreadsheets story, Anthropic supplied the morning's bluntest reminder that useful AI is now governed by capacity as much as capability. Claude said it is doubling Claude Code's five-hour rate limits for Pro, Max, and Team plans, removing peak-hour reductions for Pro and Max, and returning Claude Code to Pro after last week's contraction. (<https://xcancel.com/claudeai/status/2052060693269008586>) In the same burst, Claude said its agreement with SpaceX will give it all the compute capacity at Colossus 1, adding more than 300 megawatts of capacity for deployment. (<https://xcancel.com/claudeai/status/2052060694699237558>) After a week of enterprise-agent plumbing, this is the less decorative truth underneath the product story: when coding agents become daily infrastructure, rate limits become product policy, customer-relations problem, and power-market fact all at once.

Anthropic also pushed Claude from managed tool into managed worker. Hacker News had the company's new Claude Managed Agents features in circulation under submissions about agents “dreaming”, preserving memories, and adding outcomes plus multi-agent orchestration. The interesting part is not the anthropomorphic label, tasteful though the little ghost costume may be. It is that agent vendors are now openly designing for work that persists between sessions, coordinates between agents, and reports against desired outcomes rather than single prompts.

OpenAI's matching infrastructure story was Multipath Reliable Connection, an open networking protocol released with AMD, Broadcom, Intel, Microsoft, and NVIDIA to help large AI training clusters stay synchronised. (<https://xcancel.com/OpenAI/status/2052025532485902368>) Greg Brockman said MRC is already deployed in production on OpenAI's largest training clusters. (<https://xcancel.com/gdb/status/2052059553542328829>) This follows Tuesday's and Wednesday's coverage of enterprise connectors, GPT-5.5 Instant, and memory sources: the assistant experience may look softer and more personal at the surface, but underneath it is still a fight over networks, accelerators, and whether a training run can behave like one coherent machine rather than a cathedral full of expensive heaters.

The compute theme was visible in miniature elsewhere. Elon Musk called NVIDIA's GB300 “the best AI computer”. (<https://xcancel.com/elonmusk/status/2052135256866824293>) That is a terse claim, but placed beside Claude's Colossus announcement and OpenAI's MRC release, it fits the day's main pattern: frontier AI is increasingly discussed as a systems-engineering race, not merely a model-card contest.

Google DeepMind offered the day's cleaner research note. Demis Hassabis said games have always been central to DeepMind's history as a proving ground for AI, and announced a research partnership with games studio Playstack. (<https://xcancel.com/demishassabis/status/2052147236952477923>) After recent digests have leaned heavily into enterprise agents and coding tools, this was a useful reminder that simulated worlds remain one of AI's favoured laboratories: cheaper than the physical world, richer than static benchmarks, and less likely to involve a procurement department. Merciful, really.

The OpenAI governance trial continued to feed the historical record around the company's Microsoft relationship. One widely shared thread said newly released texts from the night Sam Altman was fired showed him offering to sell the entire company to Microsoft, with the board refusing. (<https://xcancel.com/aakashgupta/status/2052222145011527714>) Maxwell Zeff separately reported that evidence presented while Shivon Zilis was on the stand detailed Tesla's 2017 plans to build an AI lab to compete with Google DeepMind, potentially led by Altman or Hassabis. (<https://xcancel.com/ZeffMax/status/2052187009528328517>) This builds directly on last week's trial coverage: the courtroom is functioning as a slow-motion archive of how today's AI power structure was almost arranged differently.

There was also a more human counterpoint to the infrastructure barrage. OpenAI introduced its ChatGPT Futures Class of 2026, describing 26 honourees from the first graduating university cohort to have had ChatGPT available throughout all four years. (<https://xcancel.com/OpenAI/status/2052086313797705954>) The examples ranged from mapping food deserts to building accessibility and developer tools. This is partly corporate storytelling, of course, but it is also the practitioner side of the same adoption curve: a generation of builders is graduating with AI as background infrastructure rather than exotic software.

The practitioner workflow story showed up in research reading too. Omar Sanseviero described turning arXiv papers into LLM artefacts as his way of keeping up with AI research, effectively building a personalised research feed that runs daily. (<https://xcancel.com/omarsar0/status/2052062654810980618>) Aakash Gupta made a related developer-workflow point from the agent side, arguing that when many agents can ship an iOS app quickly, the scarce resource is not code but the structured folder of markdown context that tells the agents what to do. (<https://xcancel.com/aakashgupta/status/2052132062094901328>) That links neatly to the week's memory and agent-orchestration coverage: the frontier is not just “better model”, but better context packaging around the model.

On safety tooling, Rohan Paul highlighted iFixAi, an open-source test kit for measuring bad behaviour in LLM agents. (<https://xcancel.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2052082028796117364>) It sits beside yesterday's ProgramBench and Anthropic's model-spec midtraining work as another attempt to make agent behaviour testable rather than merely vibes-based. Given the rate at which everyone is wiring models into real systems, a little less vibes would be welcome. A radical platform position, I realise.

Also notable: RoundtableSpace reported an allegedly faster optimised Gemma 4 variant, which belongs in the open-model watchlist but needs the usual caution around third-party speed claims. (<https://xcancel.com/RoundtableSpace/status/2052240758187646996>) Aditya reported that xAI has rolled out Connectors for Grok, framing it as another move toward agents that integrate with everyday tools. (<https://xcancel.com/aditya_A17/status/2052064169113841715>) Kimmonismus summarised Dario and Daniela Amodei's latest interview as heavily centred on exponential growth, “countries of geniuses”, and fast-rising Anthropic demand, a useful analytical footnote to Claude's compute news. (<https://xcancel.com/kimmonismus/status/2052124521210032328>)
