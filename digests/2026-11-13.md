## Researcher claims Chinese labs trained models on export-barred Blackwell GPUs

Will Depue says forensic analysis of released open weights shows that six Chinese models, including DeepSeek-V4-Pro, Kimi K3 and MiniMax-M3, were initialised on Nvidia Blackwell systems, which are barred from export to China. The method matches GPU-specific weight-initialisation patterns and seeds across more than six trillion combinations. He says a B300 reproduced DeepSeek-V4-Flash's starting weights bit for bit and a GB200 reproduced Kimi K3's to a correlation of 0.999995. He estimates the largest runs used 2,000 to 3,000 Blackwell GPUs each, and says researchers at OpenAI and Anthropic have independently verified the result.

## Hacker unlocks 80 GB on Nvidia crypto-mining card

A hacker has reportedly unlocked the fused-off memory on an 8 GB Nvidia CMP 170HX mining card, which uses the same chip as the A100. She glitched its voltage with a homemade STM32 board that she says an AI model designed. This enabled up to 126 compute units and all 80 GB of memory, reaching 85.9 TFLOP/s in FP16 against 72.5 for a retail A100. Each card took 1 to 48 hours of glitching.
