## OpenAI pipeline patches 948 Linux kernel vulnerabilities

OpenAI said its security researchers built an autonomous pipeline that discovers, exploits and patches software vulnerabilities. In a single run using an upcoming cyber model, the pipeline validated and generated patches for 948 local privilege escalation vulnerabilities in the Linux kernel, at a total API cost of $1.2 million. The company said the agents also discovered and used at least 10 Linux exploitation techniques that it and the experts it consulted believe are novel.

## Anthropic uses Claude to map a bacterial vault particle structure

Anthropic's biology research team worked with Claude to determine the first 3D structure of a vault particle from bacterial life. Vault particles are large barrel-shaped protein structures, first discovered in 1986, whose function remains unknown; they were previously known only in complex organisms such as animals and plants. Anthropic said Claude cut structure determination from weeks or months to days, but also made simple mistakes that were addressed with its machine learning researchers. A preprint has been published.

## Karpathy outlines what a 1970s forecaster would need to predict modern AI

Responding to a post by Ilya Shostekofsky listing ways science fiction got AI wrong, such as expecting robots rather than disembodied minds and not anticipating art being displaced first, Andrej Karpathy said the critique is broadly right for most pre-2000 science fiction, with exceptions such as HAL. He listed the chain of inferences a 1970s forecaster would have needed: knowledge work going digital, a global internet, a vast record of human data, statistical learning in place of hand-programmed intelligence, ELIZA-like language models of extreme fidelity, and scaling laws concentrating development in a few organisations.
