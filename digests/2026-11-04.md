## OpenAI details ChatGPT and Codex work on physical engineering

OpenAI said ChatGPT and Codex are being used across mechanical design, electrical engineering and simulation, both by its own hardware teams and with industry collaborators. In the accompanying thread, the company said two staff members used GPT-6 Astra to design a prototype device for talking to Codex in under a week, with the model producing the enclosure, circuit board, interface and software. OpenAI is also working with Chip Ganassi Racing to turn 3D scans into editable CAD models and with Mazda on vehicle design through simulation, and is seeking engineering partners.

## Anthropic releases FlashProve for provable inference

Anthropic introduced FlashProve, a cryptographic method that lets developers verify whether a given response came from a given AI model, and said it is substantially more efficient than prior open-source approaches. The company listed uses such as making chat hijacking harder and letting safety evaluators confirm that the model they test is the one served to customers. The code has been open-sourced.

## Ai2 StateBench finds frontier models struggle to track state

The Allen Institute for AI released StateBench, an evaluation of whether language models can track state changes over long texts, such as objects changing hands or variables being reassigned. ## Google and Mercor launch Physical-Engineering's Last Exam

Google AI built a benchmark called Physical-Engineering's Last Exam with Mercor and practising engineers, covering 85 long-horizon tasks in mechanical, electrical, civil and structural engineering. All frontier models scored below 50% on the private test set, led by Claude Opus 5.5 at 48.3% and GPT 6 Astra at 45.8%. The top agent met 91% of individual constraints, but only 41.3% of its deliverables passed every hard safety check.
