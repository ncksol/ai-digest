# 27 June 2026

## OpenAI introduces the GPT-5.6 family in limited preview

OpenAI announced GPT-5.6 Sol, described as its next-generation frontier model, alongside GPT-5.6 Terra, a balanced model for everyday work, and GPT-5.6 Luna, a fast and affordable model for high-volume use. The company said it plans general availability in the coming weeks but, at the request of the U.S. government, is starting with a limited preview among a small group of trusted partners in Codex and the API. OpenAI said Sol is a step function better than GPT-5.5 and sets a new state of the art on the Terminal-Bench 2.1 command-line benchmark.

## Altman frames the GPT-5.6 preview as a government-requested step

Sam Altman said GPT-5.6 Sol is the same price as GPT-5.5 and that Terra delivers 5.5-level performance at half the price, then characterised the limited launch as the bad news. He said that at the request of the U.S. government the models launched in limited preview instead of the open-access release the company had planned, and that OpenAI is working with the government to reach general availability as fast as possible. Altman said the approach fits the company's long-held strategy of iterative deployment but is not quite the process it considers optimal.

## US Commerce Department lifts its export block on Anthropic's Mythos 5

A report citing Semafor said the U.S. government lifted its block on Anthropic's Mythos 5 model in a letter from Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick to Anthropic co-founder Tom Brown. The decision applies to the United States only and covers roughly 100 US institutions, including major companies and government agencies. ## Anthropic updates its Economic Index with hourly sampling and a user survey

Anthropic said it is advancing how it studies Claude's economic impact, adding hourly sampling and survey data to track how the cadences of daily life shape usage, what people produce with the model, and how perceptions of AI may be changing. The company said this edition is the first to survey Claude users, reporting that more than a third expect AI to handle most or nearly all of their work tasks within a year, and that those who delegate the most work are also the most optimistic about their pay and job security.
