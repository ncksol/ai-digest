## Karpathy describes a widening funnel in LLM understanding

Andrej Karpathy revisited an April post, saying the gap in shared understanding of LLM capability is widening into a funnel. He estimates about 6 billion people have barely used LLMs, 1-2 billion are casual free-tier users, around 20 million professionals use frontier models first-hand for math and code, and roughly 5,000 people have access to the most advanced internal systems.

## GPT-6.1 Sol default context raised to 500k in Codex

OpenAI's Thibault Sottiaux said the default context length for GPT-6.1 Sol in Codex and Work has increased to 500k tokens from 272k, an increase of about 80%. The change is intended to reduce compactions on long tasks. Users can still override the setting, up to the model's maximum of 1M tokens.

## Decision 3.0 open multimodal decision models released

Xunzhuo Liu announced Decision 3.0, a family of open multimodal foundation decision models ranging from 0.6B to 27B parameters. According to the team's evaluation, the models rank first on both Jev Decision Index boards and form a new Pareto frontier. The models are available as a Hugging Face collection.

## Typed Decision model built on EmbeddingGemma 2

Manuel Romero released Typed Decision, a model built on EmbeddingGemma 2. It takes a state and any schema of questions, then returns typed, calibrated answers without text generation. It scores 76.4% on typed decisions, about 6 points behind a 27B LLM while using roughly 100 times fewer parameters.

## Elixir creator questions fairness of an AI-built framework benchmark

José Valim examined why AI agents rejected pull requests that sped up the Elixir version of a cross-language benchmark while accepting similar changes for Go, Express and Rust. He found that each project had a different starting point, prompt and set of required verifications. Only the Elixir version was required to preserve pinned Rails reference behaviour and keep using Redis.
