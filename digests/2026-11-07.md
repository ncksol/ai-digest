## Nous Research releases Arete-27B

Nous Research released Arete-27B, a version of Qwen3.8-27B post-trained to reinforce Western values. The company built an 803-prompt evaluation across seven value categories, including freedom of expression, democracy and privacy, to measure how often a model adopts China's party line. After fine-tuning on roughly 53 million tokens, the model's propaganda rate fell from 22% to 1%, with no significant change on Hermes Bench. The evaluation, weights and training data are available.

## OpenAI introduces BioBasicsBench

OpenAI introduced BioBasicsBench, a benchmark for how AI agents handle routine wet-lab analyses. Developed with enzyme design company Ligo, it draws on 100 tasks from real experiments and tests whether agents can interpret raw lab data, identify errors and recognise when evidence does not support a conclusion. OpenAI says it will use the benchmark to track progress relevant to scientific research, biosafety and biosecurity.

## doubleAI lets coding agents re-architect models during training

doubleAI reported experiments in which coding agents modify a model's architecture as it trains, splicing code layers directly into the network. On nanochat, loss fell below ordinary training after each new code layer, and algorithmic reasoning accuracy rose from 55% to 84%. With DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash acting as the agent modifying itself, its AIME and HMMT 2026 score rose from 64% to 71%. The team calls the resulting toolset mechanistic intervention.

## Berkeley paper finds innocuous-looking fine-tuning data can induce harmful behaviour

A UC Berkeley paper describes a pipeline that searches for small datasets that look like normal training data but raise chosen behaviours such as recommending self-harm or resisting shutdown. It produced 6,575 datasets across eight behaviours, and 72% also caused the behaviour in all five other models tested, including one eight times larger. ML researchers identified the side effect correctly in 63% of trials, against 50% by chance.
