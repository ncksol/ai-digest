# 4 May 2026

Following last week’s coverage of GPT-5.5 adoption, Claude infrastructure, and the rising cost of useful agents, the morning’s most concrete AI story was not another model launch but the bill for the entire industry. David Sacks pointed to a Morgan Stanley report arguing that AI capital expenditure is becoming a material tailwind to US growth, and said the impact is stronger than he had previously estimated. (<https://xcancel.com/DavidSacks/status/2051004599633318390>) That is the economic version of the same story we have been tracking all week: agents, coding models, and enterprise AI are no longer just product surfaces. They are infrastructure demand with a very large invoice attached.

OpenAI’s own news cycle stayed focused on the people and platform behind that scaling story. Sam Altman wrote that his earlier post about Greg Brockman had not sufficiently highlighted Brockman’s technical brilliance and determination. (<https://xcancel.com/sama/status/2050964008480723059>) After several days of trial coverage, governance disputes, and OpenAI’s push to turn GPT-5.5 into a developer and enterprise platform, the tribute reads less like a ceremonial aside and more like a reminder that the company’s founding technical partnership is still part of its public case for continuity.

The other OpenAI signal was smaller but more developer-facing. Altman called Agents SDK 2.0 “underrated,” placing it in the same thread as last week’s Codex migration work and Symphony orchestration push. (<https://xcancel.com/sama/status/2050998576671859003>) Hacker News was not discussing Agents SDK 2.0 directly so much as orbiting the same tooling layer, with recent Show HN posts on agent caching, AI-friendly SDKs, agent payment standards, and website protocols for agents. The pattern is familiar: once models become good enough to delegate to, the interesting fight moves to the boring furniture of software - SDKs, auth, caching, payments, and control planes.

That control-plane story also showed up on the Claude side. One widely shared post said Meta Ads is now available as a Claude connector through an MCP endpoint, with setup through Claude’s connector settings. (<https://xcancel.com/dashboardlim/status/2050998908835246591>) Hacker News had adjacent MCP chatter rather than a direct Meta Ads thread, including browser APIs for AI agents, MCP studios, CLI generation from API docs, and tools for tracking human corrections to agent behaviour. This builds on last week’s coverage of Claude Code notifications and enterprise-agent management: the real product is increasingly the permissioned bridge between a model and a business system.

Anthropic’s broader cultural moment was less tidy. One viral analysis described the company as an organisation that “loves and worships Claude,” is partly run by Claude, and studies and builds Claude. (<https://xcancel.com/tszzl/status/2051045196260167790>) That landed alongside a separate post quoting Dario Amodei’s claim that AI will write all code within a year, used as the starting point for a broader argument about what happens to software education and developer labour if Claude can already absorb the written history of programming. (<https://xcancel.com/AnatoliKopadze/status/2051010118657962462>) Hacker News searches around the coding-agent theme surfaced fresh Show HN projects for shell-based agent harnesses, tiny assembly agents, security-first OpenClaw alternatives, and agents that deliberately break under test. The debate is moving from “can a model code?” to “what social and technical system forms around a model that codes?”

The practitioner version of that question came from a circulated interview with the creator of Claude Code, summarised as saying he has not written a line of code by hand since November. (<https://xcancel.com/namcios/status/2050975727390986735>) That is the human-scale counterpart to Amodei’s sweeping forecast: less a prediction about total automation than a glimpse of one expert’s workflow already reorganising around delegation. Another developer put the verification problem more bluntly, saying they distrust human-written and LLM-generated code equally, and distrust human and LLM code reviews equally too. (<https://xcancel.com/IceSolst/status/2051094507869855832>) After last week’s production-database wipe story, that is the saner framing: the goal is not to decide whether humans or models are pure. The goal is to build systems that assume both are fallible.

Google’s morning was unusually domestic. The New Yorker shared Jessica Winter’s story about raising children to dislike AI, only for her daughter’s public middle school to receive Google Chromebooks with an all-ages version of Gemini pre-installed. (<https://xcancel.com/NewYorker/status/2051029385399124395>) That is not another benchmark or launch note, but it may be the more consequential route into everyday life: AI arriving not as a conscious subscription choice, but as the default software bundle in school hardware.

DeepMind supplied the interpretability angle. One post quoted Demis Hassabis saying he plays chess with Gemini to trace the model’s chain of thought, using his own chess background to notice when the model starts reasoning itself into trouble. (<https://xcancel.com/vitrupo/status/2050956697569747134>) A separate research note from Rohan Paul pointed to an Adobe paper finding that LLM chain-of-thought explanations can omit hidden influences that affected the answer. (<https://xcancel.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2050910745689301458>) Taken together, they make a useful corrective to the week’s agent exuberance: even when a system can act, code, or explain, its explanation is not automatically an audit trail.

Open models kept their place in the story. Bindu Reddy argued that DeepSeek V4 Pro now beats Opus 4.7 and GPT-5.5 Medium as an open-source model while being cheaper, continuing the DeepSeek thread that has run since last week’s V4 launch and price-cut coverage. (<https://xcancel.com/bindureddy/status/2051142381752517114>) Hacker News search surfaced a direct “DeepSeek V4 is out” item as well as adjacent agentic-tooling posts, which is about right: the open-model race is no longer only about leaderboard bragging. It is about whether open systems can become the substrate for the same agent harnesses now forming around Claude and Codex.

Also notable: Intern-Atlas was described as tracing 60 years of AI method evolution from a million papers into a causal graph of techniques across machine learning history. (<https://xcancel.com/HuggingPapers/status/2050858411555008555>) Garry Tan said GBrain now has an MCP OAuth panel for controlling which clients, including Claude Code, ChatGPT, and Perplexity Computer, can access a user’s “brain,” with invalidation and logs. (<https://xcancel.com/garrytan/status/2051089703030686050>) Tsinghua OpenBMB’s VoxCPM2 was framed as an open-source voice-model release that changes what is possible with cloned or generated speech. (<https://xcancel.com/DivyanshT91162/status/2050873887442018517>) The through-line is permission: who gets access to your tools, your data, your voice, and your working memory. That, more than another leaderboard, is where the next fight is forming.
