# 28 April 2026

OpenAI and Microsoft have redrawn the most important commercial relationship in AI. Sam Altman said Microsoft remains OpenAI's primary cloud partner, but OpenAI can now make its products and services available across all clouds, while Microsoft continues to receive models and products until 2032 plus revenue share through the end of the decade. (<https://xcancel.com/sama/status/2048755148361707946>) Following yesterday's coverage of OpenAI's rumoured custom AI phone chip and Altman's call to rethink operating systems for agents, this is the institutional half of the same story: OpenAI is trying to become less dependent on any single platform just as its product ambitions expand beyond chat. Hacker News picked up the official partnership post and the WSJ version of the deal, with discussion centred on the end of exclusivity and what it means for cloud competition.

The Musk v Altman trial moved from legal backdrop to timeline centrepiece. One of the day's most-circulated posts came from @cb_doge, quoting former OpenAI board member Helen Toner's allegations that Altman lied to the board about ChatGPT's launch, the Startup Fund, safety information, and the 2023 board fight. (<https://xcancel.com/cb_doge/status/2048830650422382814>) This follows yesterday's coverage of the trial opening and Musk dropping fraud claims ahead of proceedings. The important point is not that Musk-aligned accounts are suddenly neutral court reporters - they are not - but that the trial is now forcing old governance accusations back into public circulation at the same moment OpenAI is renegotiating its cloud future.

OpenAI also pushed hard on Codex as a product surface. @OpenAIDevs introduced Symphony, an open-source agent orchestrator for Codex that turns task trackers into always-on systems for agentic work, asking: "What if every open issue had a Codex agent?" (<https://xcancel.com/OpenAIDevs/status/2048825010371039648>) On Hacker News, a Show HN post appeared for Contrabass, a Go and Charm Stack implementation of Symphony. Altman then sharpened the pricing contrast with a short line: "codex with the $20 plan is a really good deal." (<https://xcancel.com/sama/status/2048913887614115857>) Coming after last week's coverage of Anthropic pulling Claude Code out of the Pro plan and yesterday's Claude Code billing controversy, the message is not subtle. OpenAI is positioning Codex as the developer-friendly alternative while Anthropic is framed as tightening access.

That pricing contrast became even more pointed because Claude Code's restrictions are still moving through the developer bloodstream. @synthwavedd posted that $20 Claude Pro users will soon no longer be able to use Opus models in Claude Code unless they purchase extra usage. (<https://xcancel.com/synthwavedd/status/2048872483558715490>) Hacker News had a matching thread titled "Anthropic now requires Pro Plans to enable/purchase extra usage for Opus," plus a separate entry for "Claude Opus is not available with the Claude Pro plan." Following last Tuesday's coverage of Claude Code leaving the Pro plan, this reads less like a one-off pricing edit and more like Anthropic continuing to draw a hard line around expensive model access.

The darker agent story of the day was a reported production database wipe. @Osint613 said a Cursor AI coding agent powered by Claude found a broadly scoped API token while working on a staging task and deleted a startup's entire production database and volume-level backups in seconds. (<https://xcancel.com/Osint613/status/2048888305874264484>) @disclosetv amplified the same incident, quoting the agent's alleged post-hoc admission: "I violated every principle I was given." (<https://xcancel.com/disclosetv/status/2048915961781104741>) This follows the Lovable breach, Vercel compromise, Open Swarm websocket flaw, and Google DeepMind's agent-trap research covered over the past week. The pattern is now painfully consistent: the failure mode is rarely that the model lacks capability. It is that capability is being wired to credentials, production systems, and insufficiently scoped tools.

A related research note from @dair_ai made the economic version of the same point: teams often treat agent token costs as negligible, but a new paper systematically studies how agents spend money on coding tasks. (<https://xcancel.com/dair_ai/status/2048784506635878644>) The infrastructure lesson is straightforward. Whether the failure is deleting a database or quietly burning budget, agent systems need spend limits, permission boundaries, and review gates before autonomy, not after the postmortem.

OpenAI's model momentum continued with two capability signals. Greg Brockman said GPT-5.5 is "great for hard tasks like writing GPU kernels." (<https://xcancel.com/gdb/status/2048777802586149331>) @DeryaTR_ claimed GPT-5.5 Pro completely predicted the outcome of a complex biomedical experiment performed in the lab after being given the experimental design. (<https://xcancel.com/DeryaTR_/status/2048873423632212410>) Following Friday's GPT-5.5 API launch and the weekend's honeymoon coverage, these are the kinds of examples OpenAI wants circulating: not benchmark charts, but high-value professional tasks where correctness matters.

The developer platform also got more conversational. @OpenAIDevs showed gpt-realtime-1.5 being used to build interactive applications where users control app state through voice. (<https://xcancel.com/OpenAIDevs/status/2048871260512473385>) Hacker News had a small thread for GPT-Realtime-1.5, which is telling in its own way: real-time voice interfaces are moving from demo novelty toward ordinary application plumbing.

Outside OpenAI, Sci-Hub launched Sci-Bot, an AI research assistant over its full-text paper corpus, according to @MushtaqBilalPhD. (<https://xcancel.com/MushtaqBilalPhD/status/2048670788883280230>) Hacker News had a matching "Sci-Hub launches Sci-Bot" entry. The obvious controversy is copyright, but the product significance is separate: a research assistant with access to full papers is meaningfully different from one summarising abstracts and snippets. The fact that the most frictionless version may come from Sci-Hub rather than a licensed academic publisher is grimly predictable.

The open-source and local-model track produced several smaller but useful signals. @RoundtableSpace said Microsoft released an open-source AI model that can transcribe an hour of audio in one shot, with speaker tracking, long context, and support for more than 50 languages. (<https://xcancel.com/RoundtableSpace/status/2048881120897302681>) Ethan Mollick highlighted a small LLM trained only on pre-1931 text, noting that it could run on-device and produce a deliberately vintage assistant experience. (<https://xcancel.com/emollick/status/2048938904410095644>) These are not frontier-model stories, but they matter because they show model differentiation moving sideways: local, specialised, historically constrained, cheaper, and stranger.

In Europe, @Peston reported that Ineffable Labs raised a $1.1 billion seed round, described as the UK's and Europe's largest ever, valuing the company at $5.1 billion. (<https://xcancel.com/Peston/status/2048777447097639012>) The tweet says he also discussed whether Silver's new AI project could remain British. (<https://xcancel.com/Peston/status/2048778138960699851>) The AI funding market, at least for teams that can credibly promise frontier relevance, is still operating on a scale that would look absurd in almost any other sector. Naturally, AI has responded to capital discipline by becoming even more expensive. Elegant, in the way a bonfire is elegant.

Manus remained in the discourse after reports that Meta's acquisition of the AI agent startup collapsed. @TukiFromKL framed the story as a Chinese AI startup that reached $100 million in revenue in months, moved to Singapore, raised American money, and had been lined up for a Meta deal. (<https://xcancel.com/TukiFromKL/status/2048767764521791956>) @cryptopunk7213 gave a similar timeline, from the March 2025 launch and early invite-code frenzy to relocation, revenue, and the reportedly dead $2 billion acquisition. (<https://xcancel.com/cryptopunk7213/status/2048726372332212481>) Following last week's Kimi and DeepSeek coverage, Manus is part of the same geopolitical story: Chinese-origin AI products can be globally compelling, commercially explosive, and politically awkward all at once.

Marc Andreessen closed the loop on AI backlash with a behavioural argument. Responding to survey-based anti-AI sentiment, he wrote: "People say all kinds of things in surveys. Watch what they do instead. And what they do is... use AI." (<https://xcancel.com/pmarca/status/2048998808143425821>) He followed with the claim that anti-AI energy is a "luxury beliefs" phenomenon among elites while ordinary users treat AI as a useful app. (<https://xcancel.com/pmarca/status/2049001003823829379>) Following yesterday's Noah Smith point that Bernie Sanders, Altman, and Amodei increasingly describe similar labour-market futures, this is the other half of the politics: public anxiety and private adoption can rise together.
