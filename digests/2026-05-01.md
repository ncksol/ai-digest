# 1 May 2026

The centre of gravity moved from general coding agents to security agents overnight. Claude Security entered public beta for Claude Enterprise customers, with Anthropic saying it scans codebases for vulnerabilities, validates findings to reduce false positives, and suggests patches for review. (<https://xcancel.com/claudeai/status/2049898739783897537>) The reaction was immediate and blunt: one widely shared post framed it as Claude replacing cybersecurity jobs. (<https://xcancel.com/cgtwts/status/2049900395129155832>) Following yesterday's coverage of GPT-5.5-Cyber, David Sacks tried to normalise the shift, arguing that Mythos is simply the first of many models able to automate cyber tasks, and that OpenAI's GPT-5.5-Cyber and Chinese frontier models are moving in the same direction. (<https://xcancel.com/DavidSacks/status/2049907993588769006>) The through-line is not subtle: cyber is becoming the next high-stakes proving ground for agentic models.

OpenAI pushed the same theme from the productivity side. Sam Altman said Codex had received a big upgrade and encouraged people to try it for non-coding computer work. (<https://xcancel.com/sama/status/2049946120441520624>) OpenAI's newsroom account made the pitch more broadly, saying Codex is useful even for people without a developer background and should be thought of as a tool for daily tasks, not just programming. (<https://xcancel.com/OpenAINewsroom/status/2049982799172796614>) Following this week's Codex coverage - browser use, approval gates, hooks, and developer rollouts - OpenAI is clearly trying to move Codex out of the engineering corner and into general-purpose software operation.

That expansion makes the governance fight around OpenAI harder to treat as background noise. A viral trial-related post resurfaced a 2015 exchange in which Elon Musk corrected Sam Altman for calling OpenAI a “company”, saying it was structured as a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. (<https://xcancel.com/cyrilXBT/status/2049782623464198591>) Following this week's courtroom coverage, the old nonprofit-versus-company argument is now running directly alongside OpenAI's push into cyber, agents, and enterprise software control.

Anthropic's military-policy problem also stayed live. Polymarket reported that US defence secretary Pete Hegseth called Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei an “ideological lunatic” who should not be involved in classified decision-making. (<https://xcancel.com/Polymarket/status/2049925069724385366>) That places Anthropic in an awkward split-screen: shipping Claude Security for enterprise customers while being publicly attacked over whether its leadership belongs near classified military uses.

The open-model story kept gaining infrastructure, not just vibes. NVIDIA said SGLang is reaching fast DeepSeek-V4 decoding with roughly million-token context on Blackwell, crediting LMSYS optimisations for the model's hybrid sparse architecture. (<https://xcancel.com/NVIDIAAI/status/2049964864240791877>) Omar Khattab said he had been testing DeepSeek-V4-Pro with the Pi coding agent and was “mindblown” by how well it worked out of the box, including building an LLM wiki through Fireworks inference. (<https://xcancel.com/omarsar0/status/2050009901234282649>) Xiaomi's MiMo-V2.5-Pro was also described as an open-source model aimed at agents, coding, OpenClaw, Hermes Agent, and Claude Code-style workflows. (<https://xcancel.com/itsPaulAi/status/2049861770282443024>) Following the past week of DeepSeek V4 coverage, the question is no longer whether open models are interesting. It is how quickly the harnesses and inference stacks around them become boringly usable.

There was a useful practitioner counterweight to the frontier-lab noise. One developer described giving his nephew an RTX 3090 and a local Qwen 3.6 27B setup so he could build with “thinking power that lives at home, not on someone else's cloud.” (<https://xcancel.com/sudoingX/status/2049946621635600495>) Another post argued that people should distinguish generative AI from local offline machine learning, praising a free open-source tool that does not regenerate anything. (<https://xcancel.com/SMOUSE_CG/status/2049930415549383036>) The frontier race is loud, but the cultural shift may be just as important at the edges: capable models becoming domestic infrastructure.

Karpathy supplied the day's broader frame. In notes from a Sequoia Ascent fireside chat, he argued that LLMs are about more than speeding up existing work such as coding, pointing instead to new software shapes including apps that can be fully engulfed by generation. (<https://xcancel.com/karpathy/status/2049903821095354523>) He also resurfaced a quote he said he has been citing often, making his own feed part reading list, part operating manual. (<https://xcancel.com/karpathy/status/2049907410303865030>) Andrew Ng made a similar educational point from a different angle, saying prompting in 2026 is very different from prompting when ChatGPT first appeared, and announcing a course intended to teach AI prompting across ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and other systems. (<https://xcancel.com/AndrewYNg/status/2049886895530967534>) The industry is not merely releasing tools; it is rewriting the basic literacy required to use them well.

Medical AI produced the most concrete science-adjacent item. Google DeepMind said its AI co-clinician matched or outperformed physicians in 68 of 140 assessed areas, including triage, while humans remained better at spotting crucial red flags and guiding physical exams. (<https://xcancel.com/GoogleDeepMind/status/2049867069093474652>) David Sinclair captured the human side of the same shift, joking that he would soon be teaching genetics to AI medical students before realising they may be teaching him. (<https://xcancel.com/davidasinclair/status/2050084062430396520>) That is the useful version of the medical-AI debate: not doctor replacement as a slogan, but the division of labour between machine breadth and human judgement.

On the knowledge-work layer, James Zou said PaperQA's paperclip now includes full papers from arXiv and PubMed Central plus a large abstract corpus, giving LLM agents indexed access to research material in one line. (<https://xcancel.com/james_y_zou/status/2049926158964477957>) Jerry Liu, meanwhile, gave the operational caveat: document-processing pipelines are hard because teams must handle rate limits, parsing failures, retries, and other failure modes that simple LLM API demos hide. (<https://xcancel.com/jerryjliu0/status/2049918509178880175>) That pairing is the AI stack in miniature: more knowledge available to agents, and more plumbing needed before anyone should trust the output.

Google and Notion rounded out the enterprise-agent beat. Google Cloud said Gemini Enterprise's enhanced Agent Designer lets users describe a goal, visualise an agent plan, then test and approve agents before they run. (<https://xcancel.com/googlecloud/status/2049927674689167384>) Notion listed a month of AI releases including AI Autofill, native custom agents on mobile, Agent 2.0, calendar tools, mail tools, and a sidebar agent. (<https://xcancel.com/NotionHQ/status/2049924580484235461>) The common pattern is supervision: agents are being sold less as magic employees and more as inspectable workflows with human approval built in. Sensible, if slightly less glamorous. Glamour, as ever, is what happens before the incident report.
