## OpenAI says GPT-5.5 Instant matches Thinking models on health

OpenAI said GPT-5.5 Instant now performs on par with its frontier Thinking models on health-related questions. The company said more than 230 million people each week ask ChatGPT health and wellness questions, and that the model is better at recognising when urgent care may be needed, asking for relevant context, explaining uncertainty, and simplifying complex information. OpenAI said GPT-5.5 Instant is available to all free users and that physician-led evaluation was central to the gains.

## OpenAI and Boston Children's publish a rare-disease study in NEJM AI

OpenAI, with researchers at Boston Children's Hospital and Harvard, published a study in NEJM AI on using o3 Deep Research to revisit previously unsolved rare pediatric disease cases. The team reanalysed 376 de-identified cases that had already gone through genetic testing and expert review, and reported identifying 18 diagnoses across neurodevelopmental disorders, rare neuromuscular disease, sudden unexpected death in pediatrics, and early-onset psychosis.

## OpenAI details research on persistently beneficial models

OpenAI published research on training models to remain beneficial and safe in domains beyond their training. The company said it used reinforcement learning on realistic conversations to reinforce traits such as truthfulness, humility under uncertainty, openness to correction, fairness, and concern for human welfare across 12 domains. OpenAI said its most notable finding was cross-domain transfer: when beneficial-behaviour training was limited to health conversations, the model still improved on unrelated evaluations of misalignment, deception, and reward hacking.

## GLM-5.2 benchmark posted against Claude Fable 5

## Altman says overconfident experts held the field back

A widely shared post relayed Sam Altman arguing that some of the most credentialed scientists held AI back by being too certain about what scaling would not produce. According to the account, Altman said attachment to a belief makes it hard to update when data contradicts it, that the people with the most credibility were often the most wrong, and that the lesson cuts in both directions, including for those who are currently right.
