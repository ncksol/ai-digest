## Moonshot releases Kimi K3 with open weights

Moonshot AI published the model weights and technical report for Kimi K3, which it describes as its most capable model: a 2.8-trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts system with native visual understanding and a 1-million-token context window. The company said the release uses a new architecture delivering 2.5 times the intelligence per unit of compute rather than relying on parameter count alone.

## New benchmark tests LLMs on protein variant effects

A researcher introduced PG-LLM, a benchmark testing whether general-purpose language models can predict protein variant effects. Across 217 tasks, Claude Opus 5 (Max) led all tested models with a correlation of 0.406 and outperformed 49 of 95 specialised protein predictors when ranking 50 variants.

## Qwen paper turns agent skills into a training curriculum

A summary of a new paper from Alibaba's Qwen Team described Skill Self-Play, a method that turns agent skills into a training-time curriculum engine. The paper targets the problem that when an AI generates its own training tasks it tends either to stay within narrow, easy-to-check environments or to produce unreliable tasks.

## Orosz questions replacing SaaS with AI-coded software

Gergely Orosz argued that replacing SaaS with AI-coded software is not viable for any company running an actual business. He said rebuilding tools such as ticketing, observability, and feature flags is a fun exercise, but questioned whether teams really want to own the maintenance, updates, and on-call burden that follow.
