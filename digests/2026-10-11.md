## Nvidia reportedly in talks over Reflection AI

According to a Financial Times report summarised by Rohan Paul, Nvidia is in early talks to acquire or invest further in Reflection AI, an open-weight model start-up valued at $25 billion. One option is an acqui-hire in which Nvidia would hire the staff and license the technology, avoiding the antitrust review a full takeover would trigger; Nvidia used that structure for its $20 billion Groq deal. Nvidia has already invested $800 million in Reflection. Reflection recently released Beam, a sparse mixture-of-experts model with 501 billion total and 23 billion active parameters.

## Sacks backs Nadella on engineering controls for superintelligence

David Sacks endorsed a post by Microsoft chief executive Satya Nadella on superintelligence safety. Sacks argued against training models with a sense of self or a right to act as conscientious objectors, and in favour of separating the supply of intelligence from authority over it: deterministic controls, observability, privilege limits, logging and guaranteed shutdown. He said models and agents should be treated as powerful insider risks rather than moral patients.

## Mathematician says OpenAI Navier–Stokes proof holds up

A mathematician citing colleagues reviewing OpenAI’s Navier–Stokes result said the Lean formalisation compiles and so far shows no improper manipulation of the formal system. The Lean and natural-language versions follow the same strategy but state key lemmas differently, and the written proof contains errors described as apparently fixable. The post called for transparent LLM infrastructure for mathematicians without gatekeeping of access.
