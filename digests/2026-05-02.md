# 2 May 2026

OpenAI's GPT-5.5 launch story has turned from capability claims into adoption claims. One week after release, OpenAI said GPT-5.5 was already its strongest model launch, with API revenue growing faster than any previous release and Codex revenue doubling in under seven days as enterprise demand for agentic coding tools increased. (<https://xcancel.com/OpenAI/status/2050250926888468929>) Following yesterday's coverage of OpenAI trying to move Codex beyond programming and into general computer work, the company also pushed migration features for Codex: importing settings, plugins, agents, and project configuration so users can bring their existing workflow across with fewer interruptions. (<https://xcancel.com/OpenAI/status/2050290618187055175>) On Hacker News, related discussion was less about launch spectacle than tooling: threads included “Why Codex works better than Claude Code for my production monolith”, “Ask HN: Is Anybody Using Codex?”, and OpenAI's own “Harness engineering” post.

The best benchmark note of the morning cut through the victory lap. ARC Prize published its analysis of GPT-5.5 and Opus 4.7 on ARC-AGI-3, saying both systems still failed in ways that look less like missing facts and more like brittle abstraction: false world models, wrong abstraction levels from training data, and cases where the model solved a level but did not reinforce the reward. (<https://xcancel.com/arcprize/status/2050261221165989969>) Hacker News had the same ARC-AGI-3 analysis in circulation. That matters because the week's GPT-5.5 story has otherwise been told mostly through product rollouts, migration prompts, and developer enthusiasm. ARC's post is a useful reminder that “better at Codex” and “closer to general reasoning” are not the same claim.

Microsoft supplied the enterprise answer. Satya Nadella said Agent 365 is now generally available, extending identity, security, governance, and management systems to AI agents and their interactions across enterprise environments. (<https://xcancel.com/satyanadella/status/2050251014691840015>) The same Microsoft push is reaching more specialised knowledge work: Tom Warren reported that Microsoft wants lawyers to trust a Legal Agent inside Word documents, handling document edits, negotiation history, and complex legal workflows. (<https://xcancel.com/tomwarren/status/2050173598975840559>) Hacker News surfaced a related Microsoft 365 E7 transformation post and agent-to-agent commercial negotiation protocol discussion. The through-line is familiar but important: agents are being sold less as chatbots and more as managed enterprise actors.

Anthropic's developer story was quieter but still present. Claude announced that Code with Claude, its developer conference, returns next week, with sessions for both new and experienced Claude Code users. (<https://xcancel.com/claudeai/status/2050252933866930339>) Following yesterday's Claude Security coverage and this week's Claude Code pricing and reliability debates, that event now has a sharper job: Anthropic needs to show not just model capability, but workflow confidence.

The labour-market argument took a more philosophical turn. Sam Altman wrote that OpenAI wants to build tools that “augment and elevate people”, not entities that replace them. (<https://xcancel.com/sama/status/2050229058425045178>) That sat against a sharper counterpoint circulating around Jensen Huang and Dario Amodei: one post highlighted Huang calling Amodei's warnings about AI wiping out new-grad jobs “ridiculous”, then asked whether Huang is right or mainly worried about backlash hurting demand. (<https://xcancel.com/FinanceLancelot/status/2050284978177561033>) Following this week's coverage of cyber automation, coding agents, and AI inside professional workflows, the jobs debate is no longer abstract. It is now attached to the very products being shipped.

There was also a small but revealing product-culture moment around Codex. OpenAI Devs announced “Pets. Now in Codex”, with a `/pet` command to wake one. (<https://xcancel.com/OpenAIDevs/status/2050275713824211041>) Altman then called Codex pets not the most important thing OpenAI has ever done, but more useful than it seems, and invited people to try hatching one. (<https://xcancel.com/sama/status/2050304809572688289>) This is not frontier AI in the grand civilisational sense. It is software trying to make long-running agent work feel companionable, persistent, and a little less like staring at a terminal waiting for the machine to ask permission. Somewhere, Clippy is stretching.

Google's agent story arrived by leak rather than launch. Min Choi said Google had briefly exposed COSMO, describing local Gemini Nano, screen access, voice match, recall, browser agency, and deep research before it vanished. (<https://xcancel.com/minchoi/status/2050290896382353432>) Demis Hassabis also welcomed Shimon Whiteson for “multiagent work ahead”, a small personnel signal that fits the same direction. (<https://xcancel.com/demishassabis/status/2050239954765185345>) The implication is that Google's agent push is spreading across both Android surfaces and DeepMind research, even if the public packaging is still uneven.

The safety and systems crowd had the better sceptical material. Rohan Paul pointed to research arguing that current AI-agent groups cannot reliably coordinate or agree on simple decisions, warning that developers often assume adding more agents means better decisions. (<https://xcancel.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2050174644280590814>) Another post framed Google DeepMind's work on agent attack surfaces around a nasty web problem: sites can detect visiting AI agents and serve them different content, including hidden instructions and malicious commands in page or image content. (<https://xcancel.com/kyronis_talks/status/2050248374880997774>) Hacker News had adjacent practical threads, including “Ask HN: How do you run LLM Agents safely?”, “Armalo AI - The Infrastructure for Agent Networks”, and an open-sourced YAML-first AI-agent runtime. The market wants agents everywhere; the engineering reality is that coordination, permissions, and adversarial surfaces remain very much unsolved.

The research stream was not only about agents. Eric Topol pointed readers to a thread explaining a new LLM paper in Science. (<https://xcancel.com/EricTopol/status/2050232575088898304>) A separate practitioner note said GPT-5.5 had become “night and day” better than two weeks ago after recent work by Peter Steinberger and others. (<https://xcancel.com/thsottiaux/status/2050395190847066270>) These are small signals, but useful ones: capability progress is being felt not just in official model cards, but in the daily texture of people trying the tools on real work.

Finally, the governance thread continued from earlier in the week. Reuters summarised the first week of Elon Musk's lawsuit testimony against OpenAI and Sam Altman. (<https://xcancel.com/Reuters/status/2050387295161737296>) Following the previous digests on OpenAI's nonprofit origins, Microsoft cloud renegotiation, and courtroom testimony, this remains the background legal noise behind the GPT-5.5 and Codex push: OpenAI is scaling the products at the same time as its founding structure is being litigated in public.
